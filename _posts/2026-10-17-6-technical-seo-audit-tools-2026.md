Technical SEO audit tools crawl your site the way a search engine would and report what is broken. Choosing one comes down to site size and whether you need log files. This list is by Farman Rind, an [SEO and GEO consultant](https://farmanrind.com/), with crawl limits and prices read from each tool's own page on 17 October 2026.

Start with the free pair at the top. Most sites under a few hundred pages never need to go further down this list.

## What a technical audit tool finds

A crawler follows the links on your site and records what it sees: status codes, redirects, canonicals, titles, headings, indexability and page speed signals. It turns a vague worry into a list you can work through.

What it cannot do is tell you which problems matter. A crawler will happily report 400 issues on a 30-page site, most of them harmless. The judgement about what to fix first is still yours, and it usually starts with whether Google can reach and index the pages that make you money.

The three questions worth answering in any audit:

- Can the pages that matter be crawled and indexed?
- Does each one have a canonical that points at itself?
- Does the mobile version carry the same content as the desktop version?

## 1. Google Search Console

Search Console is free and comes from the source, which makes it the first stop rather than an afterthought. Its Page indexing report lists which pages are indexed and which are not, with a reason attached to each excluded group. No third-party crawler can tell you that, because no third-party crawler knows what Google decided.

Google describes it as a free service for monitoring, maintaining and troubleshooting your site's presence in Search, covering crawling checks, indexing fixes, re-indexing requests, traffic data and alerts about indexing or spam problems.

- The only tool that reports Google's own indexing decisions
- Free with no limits or tiers
- Includes the URL Inspection tool for single-page diagnosis

**Best for:** every site, as the first check and the final confirmation.

[Google Search Console](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9128668)

## 2. Screaming Frog SEO Spider

Screaming Frog is the desktop crawler most technical SEOs open first. It runs on your own machine, which makes it fast and keeps the data local, and the free version is the full tool rather than a demo.

The free version carries a crawl limit of 500 URLs. The paid licence is £199 per year, and lifts that limit to unlimited, with a footnote that the real maximum depends on your machine's allocated memory and storage. Multi-licence pricing stays at £199 per licence per year for one to four licences. The feature lists for free and paid are otherwise identical, so the only thing you are buying is scale.

- A full crawler at no cost for sites under 500 pages
- £199 a year is the cheapest unlimited licence here
- Runs locally, so crawl speed depends on your hardware

**Best for:** sites under 500 pages free, and any size on the paid licence.

[screamingfrog.co.uk](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/pricing/)

## 3. Sitebulb

Sitebulb is the crawler for people who want the findings explained rather than just listed. Its reports prioritise issues and explain why each one matters, which makes it easier to hand an audit to someone who is not an SEO.

It comes as desktop software for Windows or Mac, with Lite covering 10,000 URLs per audit and Pro covering 500,000. Extra users on Pro start from £7 per month. Sitebulb Cloud, for teams of two or more, starts from £95 a month according to its FAQ. The monthly price fields for the desktop plans were blank when I read the page, so confirm those before budgeting. There is a 14-day free trial equivalent to Sitebulb Pro on desktop, with no credit card required.

- Prioritised findings with explanations, not raw lists
- Generous desktop crawl limits
- Desktop prices were not displayed, so ask

**Best for:** consultants and agencies who hand audits to clients.

[sitebulb.com](https://sitebulb.com/pricing/)

## 4. Ahrefs Site Audit

Ahrefs includes a site audit inside its suite, and there is a genuinely usable free tier. Ahrefs Free gives 5,000 crawl credits a month per project for site auditing, with an unlimited number of verified websites, and it needs you to verify that you own the site before it will crawl it.

Paid plans start at $29 a month for Starter, then $129 for Lite, $249 for Standard, $449 for Advanced and $1,499 for Enterprise with an annual commitment. Paying annually saves up to 17 percent. The advantage over a standalone crawler is context: the audit sits next to backlink and keyword data for the same pages.

- 5,000 crawl credits a month at no cost
- Audit findings alongside backlink and ranking data
- Site ownership verification required for the free tier

**Best for:** anyone already using Ahrefs, or auditing their own site for free.

[ahrefs.com/free](https://ahrefs.com/free)

## 5. JetOctopus

JetOctopus is built for large sites, and its distinguishing feature is log file analysis in every plan. Its Log Analyzer is described as a way to track how Google and AI bots navigate your site, which answers a question a crawler alone cannot: not what a bot could reach, but what it actually fetched.

Growth is listed at €299 or $349 a month for 500,000 crawled URLs and 2 million log lines. Pro and Ultra sit above that, up to 5 million URLs. The pricing page shows two price pairs per plan without labelling which billing period each belongs to, and carries two different feature tables with conflicting log line allowances, so confirm the current figures before buying. A free trial covers up to 10,000 crawled URLs over 7 days.

- Log analysis included at every tier
- Scales to millions of URLs
- Pricing page is ambiguous, so verify before committing

**Best for:** technical teams on large sites who need crawl and log data together.

[jetoctopus.com](https://jetoctopus.com/pricing/)

## 6. Lumar

Lumar is the enterprise crawler, and it is honest about being one. Its own site describes ultra-fast and flexible site crawling technology built for enterprise-scale websites, with an app that crawls and analyses a website at any scale.

There is no public pricing. The site says it offers flexible pricing and packaging and directs you to fill in a form for a quote. That tells you roughly where it sits: if the price mattered to you, you would not be the customer. I have left the figures out rather than repeat numbers from review sites I cannot verify.

- Built for very large, complex sites
- Quote-based pricing, no public figures
- Expect a sales process rather than a sign-up form

**Best for:** enterprise sites with a procurement process.

[lumar.io](https://www.lumar.io/pricing/)

## My verdict

For a site under 500 pages, the answer is Search Console plus the free Screaming Frog, and you are done. That pair finds nearly everything that matters on a small site, and the total cost is nothing.

Between 500 pages and a few thousand, buy the Screaming Frog licence at £199 a year. It is the best value on this page by a wide margin. Add Sitebulb if you present findings to clients and want the explanations written for you.

Above that, or when you need to know which pages bots actually fetch rather than which they could reach, JetOctopus earns its price. Lumar is for organisations where the procurement process costs more than the software.

## FAQs

**What is the best free technical SEO audit tool?**

Google Search Console, because it reports Google's own indexing decisions with a reason for each excluded page. Pair it with the free version of Screaming Frog, which crawls up to 500 URLs with no feature restrictions. Together they cover almost everything a small site needs, at no cost.

**How often should I run a technical audit?**

Once a quarter for a stable site, and after every migration, redesign or template change. Check the Page indexing report in Search Console monthly, since that is where new problems surface first. A full crawl every week on an unchanged site tells you nothing new and wastes your time.

**Do I need a crawler if I have Search Console?**

They answer different questions. Search Console tells you what Google decided about pages it found. A crawler tells you what is on your site, including pages Google has not reached and links that are broken. Most audits need both, which is why the two free tools at the top of this list work so well together.

**What is log file analysis and do I need it?**

Log files record every request your server receives, including from search engine and AI bots. Analysis shows what bots actually fetched, rather than what they theoretically could reach. On a small site it is overkill. On a large site it is often the only way to find out why sections are not being crawled.

**Is a paid crawler worth it for a small site?**

Usually not. The free version of Screaming Frog crawls 500 URLs with the same features as the paid licence, and Ahrefs Free gives 5,000 crawl credits a month for your own verified sites. Pay when your site outgrows those limits, or when a client needs a report you cannot produce by hand.
