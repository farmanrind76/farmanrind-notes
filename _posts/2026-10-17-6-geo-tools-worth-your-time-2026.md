GEO tools tell you whether AI assistants name your brand when buyers ask about your category. [Farman Rind](https://farmanrind.com/) tested the market for this list, and the six tools below are the ones with a real product and a price you can check. Prices were read from each company's own pricing page on 17 October 2026.

Two warnings before the list. First, this market is young, and several of the better-known names will not publish a price at all. Second, no tool can promise you a citation. They measure. The work of earning the mention is still yours.

## How I picked these six

I only included a tool if I could open its own site and confirm what it does. Where a company publishes no price, I say so rather than repeating a figure from a review site. Several tools you will see on other lists are missing here for exactly that reason.

The test for each one was simple:

- Does it track brand mentions across named AI platforms?
- Does it say which platforms, rather than "all AI"?
- Is there a price, a trial or a clear route to one?
- Does it do something a spreadsheet and an hour a month cannot?

## 1. Semrush

Semrush is the easiest starting point, because AI visibility now sits inside a suite many teams already pay for. Its free plan lists AI Visibility features alongside one project and ten reports a day, which is enough to see whether your brand shows up at all before you spend anything.

Paid plans on the SEO and AI Search tab start at $117.33 a month billed annually, shown against $139 monthly. Starter is $165.17 billed annually against $199 monthly, and Pro+ is $248.17 against $299. Advanced is quoted on request.

- AI visibility reporting next to ordinary keyword and backlink data
- A free tier that is genuinely usable for a first look
- One login for SEO and AI work

**Best for:** teams already paying for an SEO suite who want AI tracking without a second subscription.

[semrush.com](https://www.semrush.com/prices/)

## 2. Otterly.AI

Otterly.AI is the clearest small-budget entry point, and it is unusually specific about which engines it covers. The base plans track four AI search engines: ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot. Claude, Google AI Mode and Gemini are sold as paid add-ons, which is worth knowing before you buy.

Pricing is listed in US dollars, excluding tax: Lite at $29 a month, Standard at $189 and Premium at $489, with custom pricing above that. The page also displays a second, lower set of figures without labelling the billing period, so confirm which applies before you commit.

- Named engines rather than vague coverage
- A genuinely cheap first tier
- Add-ons let you pay only for the platforms you care about

**Best for:** a small business or consultant testing AI visibility for the first time.

[otterly.ai](https://otterly.ai/pricing/)

## 3. Peec AI

Peec AI is built for marketing teams and SEO agencies rather than solo users. It monitors how brands appear in AI assistant answers, compares you against competitors and suggests ways to improve that presence. Its own site says it covers ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini.

The plans are Starter, Pro, Advanced and Enterprise. No prices are published for any of them, so budget by asking rather than by guessing. I have left the figures out here rather than repeat numbers I could not verify on their site.

- Competitor comparison built in, not bolted on
- Aimed at teams reporting to a client or a board
- Three major assistants covered

**Best for:** agencies that need a client-ready view of AI visibility.

[peec.ai](https://peec.ai/pricing)

## 4. Profound

Profound is the enterprise end of this market. It runs structured prompts across AI platforms and analyses how a brand appears, including citations, sentiment, ranking and competitive presence. It also sells agents for recurring marketing workflows, which is a different product from pure monitoring.

There is a free trial. Beyond that, pricing is custom and quoted per company. The site mentions a self-serve agency plan with 400 credits a month per client workspace, again without a public price.

- Sentiment and citation analysis, not just a mention count
- Workflow automation alongside reporting
- Free trial, so you can judge it before any sales call

**Best for:** larger brands with a budget holder who expects a demo and a contract.

[tryprofound.com](https://www.tryprofound.com/pricing)

## 5. SE Ranking

SE Ranking is interesting because it counts AI prompts in the same plan as ordinary keywords, which makes the cost of AI tracking easy to see. Core is $103.20 a month, shown against $129, and covers 2,000 keywords and 100 prompts tracked daily. Growth is $223.20 against $279, with 5,000 keywords and 250 prompts. Enterprise is quoted on request.

The page carries monthly and annual toggles and a 20 percent annual saving, without saying which billing period the displayed prices reflect, so check at the cart.

- Prompts and keywords in one subscription
- Clear daily tracking limits
- Priced below the better-known suites

**Best for:** anyone who wants AI prompt tracking and classic rank tracking on one bill.

[seranking.com](https://seranking.com/pricing/)

## 6. JetOctopus

JetOctopus is the odd one out, and it earns its place. It is a crawler and log analyser rather than a mention tracker, and its Log Analyzer module is described as a way to track how Google and AI bots navigate your site. That answers a question the mention trackers cannot: are the AI crawlers reaching your pages in the first place?

Growth is listed at €299 or $349 a month for 500,000 crawled URLs and 2 million log lines, with Pro and Ultra above that. The page shows two price pairs per plan without labelling the billing period clearly, and two different feature tables, so confirm the current figures before buying. A free trial covers up to 10,000 crawled URLs over 7 days.

- Shows which AI bots actually fetch your pages
- Log analysis included in every plan
- Built for large sites

**Best for:** technical teams on big sites who want evidence, not inference.

[jetoctopus.com](https://jetoctopus.com/pricing/)

## My verdict

Start with the free Semrush tier and a spreadsheet. Write down twenty questions your buyers would ask an AI assistant, run them once a month, and record whether you are named and who is cited instead. If that exercise shows a real gap, buy Otterly.AI at the bottom end or SE Ranking if you also need rank tracking. Only move to Peec AI or Profound when somebody else needs the report.

The tool is the cheap part. The expensive part is fixing what it finds.

## FAQs

**What is a GEO tool?**

A GEO tool monitors whether AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews mention or cite your brand when people ask questions in your category. It runs a set of prompts on a schedule and records the answers. It measures visibility. It does not create it, and no tool can promise a citation.

**Do I need a paid GEO tool to start?**

No. Semrush lists AI Visibility features on its free plan, and you can test twenty buyer questions by hand in under an hour a month. Start there. Buy a paid tool when the manual check shows a real gap, or when somebody other than you needs to see the report regularly.

**Which AI platforms should a tool cover?**

Cover the ones your buyers use. For most businesses that means ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity, with Claude and Copilot after that. Check the platform list before you buy, because coverage differs. Otterly.AI, for example, includes four engines in its base plans and sells Claude and Gemini as add-ons.

**Why do some GEO tools hide their prices?**

Most of the enterprise end of this market quotes per company, because scope varies with the number of prompts, platforms and competitors tracked. Profound and Peec AI both publish plan names without figures. That is normal for young enterprise software, but it does mean you cannot compare them on price without a sales call.

**Are GEO tools worth the money?**

They are worth it once you have something to measure and someone to report to. If you have fewer than twenty indexed pages or no organic baseline, fix that first. A tool that tells you every month that you are invisible is an expensive way to learn something a single afternoon of manual checking would have told you.
